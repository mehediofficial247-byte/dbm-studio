Hourly billing has a built-in bug: the better you get, the less you earn per project, because you finish faster. I moved to flat, scope-based pricing three years ago and haven't billed an hour since.

## The model

Every project gets a fixed price attached to a defined scope, not a time estimate.

1. Client shares their goals and constraints on a discovery call
2. I write a scope document listing exactly what's included — and what isn't
3. I attach a flat price to that scope, not an hourly estimate
4. Anything outside the written scope is a new, separately priced item

Clients get budget certainty. I get to keep the upside of getting faster.

## Where it goes wrong

The hard part isn't the pricing model — it's scoping tightly enough that "fixed price" doesn't quietly become "unlimited revisions." The scope document is the thing that protects both sides, and it needs to name deliverables, not vague outcomes.

> A scope that says "improve the homepage" will get renegotiated in month two. A scope that says "redesign and rebuild the homepage, up to two rounds of revisions per section" won't.

## If you're making this switch

Start by tracking your hourly rate on your next three hourly projects. Then price the next similar project at what those hours would have cost you, delivered as a single flat number. You'll likely find you were underpricing your speed the whole time.
